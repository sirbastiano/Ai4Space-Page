AI4Space Workshop Overview


The AI4Space workshop at CVPR 2026 brings together researchers, engineers, and space domain experts to address the unique challenges of deploying AI in space environments. As missions demand greater autonomy, resilience, and on-board intelligence, this workshop explores how computer vision and machine learning can enable breakthrough capabilities in spacecraft navigation, planetary exploration, satellite data analysis, and orbital operations.


AI4Space fosters collaboration between machine learning researchers and space scientists, focusing on topics such as vision-based navigation, onboard learning, sim-to-real transfer for robotics, multi-sensor fusion for Earth observation, and AI-driven mission planning—shaping the future of intelligent space exploration.

Keynote Speakers

David Rijlaarsdam

Director of Space System Engineering, Ubotica Technologies

David Rijlaarsdam manages the Space System R&D team at Ubotica, where he is responsible for applying systems engineering across the organization and for designing and operating Ubotica’s space missions. Previously he worked on applying AI to star identification algorithms at Intel Movidius, and relative visual navigation systems at ESA. His group’s work focuses on autonomy for Earth observation, sensorwebs, navigation, space situational awareness and rover systems. His team has been responsible for the system engineering for onboard processing of ESA’s PhiSat-2, CogniSAT-6, Aries SN-1, and other missions. He was PI for Ubotica for the first single sensor dynamic targeting flight ever performed, in an effort funded by NASA.

Marco Pavone

Associate Professor, Stanford University | Director of Autonomous Vehicle Research, NVIDIA

Dr. Marco Pavone is an Associate Professor of Aeronautics and Astronautics at Stanford University, where he directs the Autonomous Systems Laboratory and the Center for Automotive Research at Stanford. He is also a Distinguished Research Scientist at NVIDIA where he leads autonomous vehicle research. Before joining Stanford, he was a Research Technologist within the Robotics Section at the NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory. He received a Ph.D. degree in Aeronautics and Astronautics from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology in 2010. His main research interests are in the development of methodologies for the analysis, design, and control of autonomous systems, with an emphasis on self-driving cars, autonomous aerospace vehicles, and future mobility systems. He is a recipient of a number of awards, including a Presidential Early Career Award for Scientists and Engineers from President Barack Obama, an Office of Naval Research Young Investigator Award, a National Science Foundation Early Career (CAREER) Award, a NASA Early Career Faculty Award, and an Early-Career Spotlight Award from the Robotics Science and Systems Foundation. He was identified by the American Society for Engineering Education (ASEE) as one of America’s 20 most highly promising investigators under the age of 40. His work has been recognized with best paper nominations or awards at a number of venues, including the European Conference on Computer Vision, the IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation, the European Control Conference, the IEEE International Conference on Intelligent Transportation Systems, the Field and Service Robotics Conference, the Robotics: Science and Systems Conference, and the INFORMS Annual Meeting.

Ethan Rublee

CEO and Co-Founder, Space-ng

Ethan Rublee is Co-Founder and CEO of Space-ng, developing autonomy systems for spacecraft spanning cameras, vision-based navigation and GPU-enabled AI hardware and software. As Vision Navigation Lead for Firefly Aerospace’s Blue Ghost Mission 1, Rublee and the Space-ng team developed and deployed the real-time vision-based terrain-relative navigation system that enabled the first successful commercial lunar landing in March 2025. Rublee has over 16 years of experience in robotics and computer vision, with a focus on taking systems from research to deployed products. He is a recipient of the 2021 ICCV Helmholtz Prize for his 2011 work on ORB, a real-time feature detection and description method. A serial entrepreneur, he has founded companies across multiple domains: industrial robotics for material handling and autonomous truck unloading (Industrial Perception, acquired by Google), AI-driven tools for real-time visual effects and mixed reality (Arraiy), and agricultural robotics systems (farm-ng). He is a co-author of Kornia, an open-source differentiable computer vision library.
